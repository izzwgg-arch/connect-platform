# LOOPCOM — 15 SECOND AD SCRIPT

> **Draft — under review.** Not approved copy. See `README.md` in this folder.

Three versions. A is the recommendation. All are timed at a measured ad read
(~2.8 words/second), which leaves room to breathe instead of racing.

---

## TWO RULES FOR 15 SECONDS

**1. Never speak the phone number.**
"Eight four five, seven two three, one two one three" takes 4.5 seconds.
That is 30% of the entire ad spent on ten digits. On screen it costs nothing
and it's more accurate. Say "WhatsApp us" and let the number sit in frame.

**2. The on-screen text has to work with the sound off.**
Most WhatsApp Status views are muted. If someone watches this silently and
learns nothing, the ad failed. So the on-screen text is not a caption of the
voiceover — it's a parallel, shorter version that stands alone.

Leads with the positioning line. Broadest appeal, works for any business.

| Time | Voiceover | On screen | Visual |

**31 words.** Voiceover ends at 14.0s, leaving one full second of the number
on screen in silence. That silence is deliberate — it's the only moment the
viewer isn't being talked at, so it's the moment they read.

## WHAT I CUT, AND WHY

At 30 seconds you can have six beats. At 15 you get four, maybe five. These
went, in this order:

- **The problem setup.** Version A opens on the answer. There is no time to
  build tension and then release it, and a half-built tension is worse than
  none.
- **The feature list.** "Calls, texts, WhatsApp, team chat, and the customer
  record underneath it" is accurate and it is far too long. It became "calls,
  texts and WhatsApp" — three items, because a list of three scans instantly
  and a list of five does not.
- **The spoken phone number.** See rule 1.
- **Everything about the platform.** No mention of the record, the team chat,
  the transcription engine. At 15 seconds the ad has one job: make them
  message you. The brochure explains; the ad interrupts.

## PRODUCTION NOTES

- **Cut on the beat.** Four hard cuts at 3.0, 6.2, 10.0 and 12.5. No cross-
  dissolves — they read as soft, and the brand is not soft.
- **The last card holds still.** Everything before it can move. The final
  frame with the number does not move at all, for a full 2.5 seconds. Motion
  on a call-to-action costs you readers.
- **On-screen text is rendered, never generated.** The AI makes the plates
  behind it with nothing readable in frame. See LOOPCOM AD PROMPTS.md.
- **Type sizes:** headline 64–80px on a 1080-wide vertical frame, the price
  card larger. Nothing under 18px, ever.
- **Music:** one bed, no sting until the final card. A single hit as the logo
  lands is worth more than a track that builds for 15 seconds and arrives
  nowhere.
