E911 Disclosure

How 911 works on a VoIP phone service — and what you must know and tell your users.

Last updated: [Month DD, YYYY]

Please read this carefully. Loop provides 911 emergency calling, but VoIP 911 service works differently from traditional landline 911 and has important limitations. FCC rules require us to tell you about them, and require you to acknowledge them and inform every user of your service.

1. How 911 works on Loop

When you dial 911 from a Loop number, your call is routed to the emergency dispatch center (PSAP) that serves the physical service address registered for your line, and that registered address and your callback number are delivered to the dispatcher. Emergency responders are sent to the registered address — not necessarily to where you actually are.

2. You must register and update your address

  • You must provide an accurate physical street address (a “Registered Location”) for each user and device when service is set up.
  • If you move a device — including taking a laptop or app to another location — you must update the Registered Location in the portal or by contacting support before relying on 911 from that device.
  • Address changes may take time to propagate to emergency systems. Until confirmed, 911 calls may be routed based on the old address.

3. Mobile apps and softphones

The Loop mobile app runs on your smartphone. Where possible, dial 911 using your phone’s native dialer instead of the app — your mobile carrier's 911 service can locate you by your device's actual position. If a 911 call is placed through the app, it is treated as a VoIP call and routed based on your Registered Location.

4. Service limitations — 911 may not work if:

  • Power is out — your internet equipment, desk phone or computer has no power.
  • Internet is down — broadband outage, network congestion, or Wi-Fi failure.
  • Your service is suspended — including for non-payment.
  • You're calling from an unregistered or outdated location — responders may be sent to the wrong address.
  • Device or network problems prevent the call from being completed.

Consider maintaining an alternative means of calling 911 (such as a mobile phone) for emergencies.

5. Your obligations

  • Keep every Registered Location accurate and current.
  • Inform all users of your Loop service — employees, family members, guests — of these 911 limitations.
  • Place the provided 911 advisory labels (available on request) on or near devices using the service.

6. Fees

E911 regulatory fees are collected per line as required and itemized on your invoice.

7. Limitation of liability

To the extent permitted by law, and consistent with FCC requirements, Loop and its underlying providers are not liable for failures of emergency calling caused by circumstances beyond our control, including those described above. See the Terms of Service for the complete provision.
